Every lifter knows recovery matters. Almost nobody tracks it, because most apps make you fill in a form: open a food diary, type in grams, scroll through a sleep chart. Havyu does it differently. **Recovery is a deck of questions, and each one takes a tap.**

## What is the “Have you…?” check-in?

Open Havyu's Recovery tab and the coach asks you one thing at a time:

- *Have you logged last night's sleep?*
- *Hit your protein goal?*
- *Drunk enough water?*
- *Weighed in today?*

Each habit is a card. Answer it and the card flies off the screen, so the list gets shorter as you go. A progress track fills as you answer, and the coach counts you home: *“One to go for a perfect day.”* Answer every card and you've had a **Perfect Day**, marked in gold, because gold in Havyu always means *now*.

It's built like an inbox you clear rather than a form you complete. That's the difference between tracking recovery for a week and tracking it for a year.

## Why questions instead of forms?

Because the data only helps if you actually log it, every day. Research on behaviour change keeps finding the same thing: **self-monitoring is one of the most effective ingredients in changing a habit**, especially when it's paired with a clear goal.[^1] And habits take time to stick: in one study, new daily behaviours took a median of 66 days to become automatic.[^2]

So the check-in is built to be repeatable for months:

- **One question at a time.** No dashboard to decode before you can log.
- **A tap, not a number, wherever possible.** Exact grams and millilitres are there when you want them.
- **The coach picks the question that matters now.** Trained today? It asks about protein. First thing in the morning? It asks about sleep.
- **Steps and body weight fill themselves in** from Apple Health on iPhone or Health Connect on Android.[^3]

## Answered, not just “hit”

Havyu separates two things most trackers blur together: **whether you answered** and **whether you hit your goal**.

Your Perfect Day and your check-in streak count honest answers. Logging “I missed protein today” still counts as checking in, because a true miss is far more useful to your coach than a gap. The weekly and monthly views then show what you actually hit, habit by habit, against your own goals.

The result is recovery data you can trust, which is the only kind worth coaching from.

## How does the check-in drive progressive overload?

This is where the check-in earns its place. Muscle grows through progressive overload: adding weight when your body is ready for it. Whether you're ready depends on what happened outside the gym, which is exactly what the check-in records.

Havyu's coach reads both sides together:

- **Before you train**, Today's focus looks at what has stalled and how last night's sleep should shape the session.
- **During a session**, each lift gets a clear call: **increase**, **maintain** or **ease back**, from how that lift has been moving and how recovered you are.
- **Each week and month**, the Recovery coach asks about your weakest habit. *Have you been hitting protein this month?* So you know which habit is holding your lifts back.

A workout logger can tell you that your bench press stalled. Havyu can tell you it stalled in the weeks your protein dropped, and when it's ready to go up again.

## How do you start?

1. Download Havyu and open the Recovery tab. No account is needed.
2. Choose the habits you care about and set your goals: protein in grams, water, sleep hours, steps.
3. Connect Apple Health or Health Connect to fill in steps and body weight automatically.
4. Answer today's cards. Under a minute later, your coach has its first day of recovery data.
