Your workout is the stimulus. Whether it turns into muscle is decided in the other 23 hours. Progressive overload, adding weight over time, only works when you've recovered enough to lift more than last time. Miss recovery and the same session that should have moved stalls instead.

The good news is that recovery comes down to a handful of daily habits. Here are the five that matter most for lifters, each phrased the way Havyu's [“Have you…?” check-in](/blog/have-you-check-in) asks it.

## 1. Have you slept 7–9 hours?

Sleep is where most of the repair happens, and the evidence is stark. In a controlled trial, people on an identical diet who slept 5.5 hours lost about **60% more lean mass** than those who slept 8.5 hours.[^1] Short sleep also impairs athletic performance and recovery more broadly.[^2]

**Target:** 7–9 hours, at similar times each night. More detail in [does sleep affect muscle growth?](/guides/does-sleep-affect-muscle-growth)

## 2. Have you hit your protein?

Protein is the raw material for new muscle. The largest meta-analysis on the question found that gains from protein **plateaued at about 1.6 g per kg of body weight per day**, with an upper confidence limit of about 2.2 g/kg.[^3]

**Target:** 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight, spread across the day. For an 80 kg lifter, that's roughly 130–175 g. See [how much protein to build muscle](/guides/how-much-protein-to-build-muscle).

## 3. Have you drunk enough water?

You lift with the water in your muscles. Training while dehydrated measurably reduces strength, power and the work you can do in a session. The research is summarised in [how much water when lifting](/guides/how-much-water-when-lifting).

**Target:** arrive at the gym hydrated: pale urine, and water through the day rather than all at once afterwards.

## 4. Have you moved outside the gym?

An hour in the gym doesn't cancel a day in a chair. Daily step count is one of the strongest everyday markers of health: in a meta-analysis of 15 cohorts, more steps were linked to a lower risk of death, levelling off at about 6,000–8,000 steps a day for adults over 60 and 8,000–10,000 for younger adults.[^4]

**Target:** a daily step goal you can hit on rest days as well as training days. Havyu fills this in from Apple Health or Health Connect.

## 5. Have you trained, or rested on purpose?

Recovery isn't the absence of training; it's training placed well. Hitting each muscle group at least twice a week produces more growth than once a week,[^5] and the rest days in between are where that growth happens.

**Target:** every day is either a planned session or a planned rest day. Never a skipped one you didn't choose. More in [how many days a week to lift](/guides/how-many-days-a-week-to-lift).
